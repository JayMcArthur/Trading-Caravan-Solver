# Deferred food / gold flex design

## Verdict

The idea is viable and complements continuation-based food thresholds.

Instead of emitting one complete solver state for every food count at a Buy,
keep one symbolic state containing the still-feasible food quantities from that
specific Buy. As later nights and actions reveal what the route needed, discard
impossible quantities. The smallest surviving quantity is the committed food
floor, and its old Buy cost is the flex gold used.

At Sell, the next Buy, or the finish, resolve the symbolic state and rewrite the
old route action with that food amount.

The executable logic experiment is
[`benchmarks/prototypes/food-gold-flex-prototype.html`](../benchmarks/prototypes/food-gold-flex-prototype.html).

## Why a single reserved-gold number is insufficient

Assume an earlier Buy could have purchased 0-10 food at 3 gold each. If nothing
but fixed income and fixed costs happens, delaying the deduction works because
addition and subtraction commute. A night that proves two more food were needed
can raise the commitment by two and charge six gold to the old Buy.

Four details prevent representing that as only one scalar:

1. **Original affordability.** Income earned after the Buy cannot retroactively
   fund food at the old Buy. The original gold cap must remain attached to the
   flex.
2. **Original carrying capacity.** A Camel bought later cannot make the earlier
   Buy carry more food. The original weight cap must remain attached too.
3. **Past action affordability.** A later food commitment must still leave every
   earlier Trader, Camel, or Merchant purchase affordable in the counterfactual
   route.
4. **Interest and percentage effects.** `interest(gold - food_cost)` is not equal
   to `interest(gold) - food_cost`, especially with integer rounding. Each food
   alternative must receive the exact multiplication and rounding.

The compact logical value is therefore a monotone food/gold curve, not one
reserved balance.

## Exact symbolic representation

For one earlier Buy with a fixed cargo basket, retain:

```text
buy day
original food price and weight
original maximum food by gold
original maximum food by carrying capacity
minimum food units committed so far
surviving food-unit -> current gold/current food curve
trace token for the unresolved Buy
```

The prototype stores the curve as explicit alternatives because the largest
observed food range in the Normalia beam-1,000 profile was only 45. Production
can first retain only the food thresholds generated by the continuation model,
then intern or breakpoint-compress identical curves so each solver state stores
only a small curve identifier.

## Transitions

### At the original Buy

Fix the non-food cargo basket. Construct every feasible food threshold using
the Buy day's gold, food price, food weight, and remaining capacity. Each point
starts with the gold and inventory it would really have had after that Buy and
its end-of-day income.

### At start of day

Apply the signed food consumption to every curve point. Remove points whose food
would become negative. If the smallest surviving food quantity increases, the
difference is the newly committed food; its original price is the newly used
flex gold.

This is the user's night-time operation, represented without changing history.

### At Trader, Camel, or Merchant spending

Apply the exact cost to every surviving point and remove points that cannot
afford it. Then apply the exact crew, food-rate, capacity, or event change. A
later capacity increase does not enlarge the old Buy's food maximum.

### At Interest or a percentage disaster

Apply the exact multiplication and rounding independently to every curve point.
Do not apply the effect to a pretend all-gold balance and subtract food later.

### At Sell, next Buy, or finish

Choose the smallest surviving food quantity. With the action path and cargo
basket fixed, less food means at least as much gold and capacity, while food has
no sale value or final score in normal mode. Record that quantity against the
unresolved Buy trace token and collapse the curve to the chosen concrete state.

If the old Buy contained cargo, the next-Buy boundary cannot occur until Sell
clears the cargo lock. `bought_last` legality still applies as usual.

## What should and should not be deferred

Defer the food quantity first. Keep the cargo basket fixed at the Buy.

Food is suitable because, for a fixed action path, the minimum survivable amount
dominates every larger amount. Cargo composition is different: it changes sale
income, event exposure, carrying capacity, and which item prices matter. A
combined `(food, cargo basket)` flex is possible, but that is a purchase frontier
rather than a scalar reservation and should only follow after the food model is
validated.

The existing explicit purchase generator should remain as the control oracle
while this is developed.

## Deep module seam

Keep the symbolic rules behind one module rather than spreading special cases
through `GameSolver::expand_state`:

```cpp
class DeferredBuyModel {
public:
    FlexState open(const SolverState& before_buy, const buy_list& fixed_cargo);
    FlexState apply(const FlexState& state, const SolverAction& action);
    ResolvedBuy resolve(const FlexState& state);
};
```

The interface hides original-day limits, signed food, exact gold transforms,
curve compression, and trace rewriting. Tests and the solver cross the same
seam.

## How it combines with food-continuation thresholds

The continuation generator answers: “Which food amounts enable a genuinely new
action pattern?” The flex answers: “Can these amounts remain unresolved while
we follow one of those patterns?”

Use them together:

1. generate threshold food amounts and witness continuations;
2. group thresholds that share the same fixed cargo basket;
3. carry the group as one food/gold flex state;
4. filter it as actions occur; and
5. resolve it at a Buy-cycle boundary.

This avoids trying every integer food count and avoids prematurely choosing
between food and retained gold.

## Likely benefit

The current Normalia beam-1,000 profile generated 25,461 Buy states, 61.2% of
all generated states. It examined 15,122 raw food positions, with ranges as wide
as 45, and saw 13,326 duplicate Buy emissions. A flex can collapse food variants
that share a cargo basket and action history, while threshold generation removes
counts that enable no new continuation.

The exact reduction is not known yet because the present purchase loop already
skips some ranges when the same cargo result repeats. A shadow counter must
measure:

- explicit food states versus flex groups;
- average and maximum thresholds per flex;
- curve points after each action day;
- flexes invalidated by food, gold, and original capacity;
- resolutions at Sell, next Buy, Interest-heavy paths, and finish; and
- whether expanded flex states exactly equal explicit reference states.

For short runs, purchase-tree deserialization still dominates wall time. The
flex is aimed at long and proof-oriented searches where frontier size and Buy
branching dominate.

## Validation gates

1. Keep current explicit food states as the oracle.
2. In shadow mode, expand every flex at each boundary and compare the full set of
   concrete states with the oracle.
3. Cover additive income/cost sequences, crew growth, original capacity,
   Cornucopia/Oasis/Camelization, signed consumption, Interest rounding, and
   percentage disasters.
4. Replay every verified route after trace resolution and require exactly the
   same Buy quantities and score.
5. Run Normalia beams 1,000 through 100,000 and the 22-town beam-1,000 matrix.
6. Enable production replacement only after zero concrete-state differences;
   do not use a score-only comparison.

This is a promising compression model, but it should enter C++ as a shadow
implementation first. A bug in historical gold or capacity constraints could
create routes the real game never allowed.
